The analysts involved in the prepping, processing, aliquoting, and reading of the samples - Cuong Du (CCD),Devanshi 
Shah (DS) and America Alanis (ALA) - were interviewed comprehensively. Their answers are recorded throughout this 
document. This investigation was performed as per SOP 2.600.069 - Sterility Test Out-of-Specification (OOS) 
Investigation Procedure.
 
Upon arrival, the sample was stored in accordance with the Client's instructions. Both the sample prepping analyst - 
CCD - and the processing analyst - DS - verified the sample's integrity throughout both the preparation and processing 
stages. No leaks or turbidity were observed at any point, verifying the integrity of the sample.
 
All reagents and supplies mentioned in the material section above were stored according to the suppliers' 
recommendations, and their integrity was visually verified before utilization. Moreover all reagents and supplies had 
valid expiration dates. The functionality of all equipment was confirmed by reviewing data generated by our 
comprehensive in-house continuous monitoring system.
 
The sample was processed within the ISO 5 Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC), E001314, situated in the innermost 
cleanroom (Suite 115B), which connects to the intermediate ISO 7 buffer room (Suite 115A). This buffer room, in turn, 
leads to the outermost ISO 8 Anteroom (Suite 115). Furthermore, a positive air pressure system is maintained between 
the suites, ensuring airflow from the innermost ISO 7 cleanroom to the intermediate ISO 7 buffer room and finally to the 
outermost ISO 8 Anteroom.
 
The innermost ISO 7 Cleanroom Suite 115B, and the ISO 5 BSC E001314 within the Suite 115B were thoroughly 
cleaned and prepared before initiating the testing by analyst DS as per SOP 2.600.002 (Environmental Monitoring of 
the Clean Room Facility), and SOP 2.600.018 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology). Similarly, for 
Celsis aliquoting, the ISO 7 Cleanroom 114A, and the ISO 5 BSC E001798 within the ISO 7 Cleanroom 114A were 
thoroughly cleaned and prepared by aliquoting analyst ALA, before initiating the testing as per SOP 2.600.002 and SOP 
2.600.018. Both the BSCs, E001314 in suite 115B and E001798 in suite 114A, were certified and approved by the 
Engineering and Quality Assurance teams prior to use.
 
On 07-May-2026, DS confirmed that each sample vial for ETX-260505-0317, was sprayed with an acidified bleach 
disinfectant, placed into pre-disinfected bins, and allowed a 10-minute contact time. Following initial disinfection, the 
bins were transferred to the ISO 8 anteroom (Suite 115), where the vials underwent a second disinfection using 
acidified bleach, again with a 10-minute contact time. Subsequently, the vials were moved into the ISO 7 cleanroom 
(Suite 115B). Inside this cleanroom, the processing analyst, DS, performed a final disinfection step, allowing an 
additional 10-minute contact time. Once fully disinfected, the vials were transferred into the ISO 5 BSC E001314. Inside 
the BSC, the vials were placed on the disinfected work surface, aseptically opened, and tested in accordance with SOP 
2.600.059 (Celsis Sterility Testing). Following testing, the media bottles were subsequently transferred into designated 
incubators, E001356 and E001357, to initiate incubation.

Upon completion of incubation on 14 May 2026, TSB & FTM bottles for  ETX-260505-0317 were 
disinfected and transferred to the middle ISO 7 buffer room (Suite 114A) for aliquoting step per SOP 
2.600.059 (Celsis Sterility Testing). In Suite 114A, the media bottles were disinfected one more time 
before transferring them to the ISO 5 BSC E001798 located in Suite 114A. In ISO 5 BSC E001798, the 
sample was aliquoted into assay cuvettes by analyst ALA.
 
After aliquoting, Celsis Sterility Reading was performed in accordance with SOP 2.600.059 by analyst 
ALA. 
 
Following the reading, sample  ETX-260505-0317 was found to be positive in the FTM media bottle. The 
average Relative Luminescence Units (RLU) from the duplicate reading tube, originating from the FTM 
sample bottle for each sample, exceeded the average RLU of the FTM negative control, confirming a 
positive result. It is to be noted that the %CV from the duplicate FTM media bottles readings was greater 
than 30%. However, both duplicate tubes reached the instrument’s upper detection limit, resulting in an 
‘overload’ readings. All Daily Controls, including the Instrument Blank, Reagent Blank, and ATP Positive 
Control, were within the defined specifications, each with a %CV below 30%. 
 
Following the OOS result, the positive FTM bottle for the sample was submitted for Differential Staining 
and Microbial Identification under ETX-260514-0573. Microbial growth was identified as Staphylococcus 
Capitis (Gram (+) cocci). 
 
The culture media utilized were within their expiry period. The negative culture media bottles were handled
, processed, and incubated in a manner identical to that of actual samples. No microbial growth was 
observed in the corresponding negative control.
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the outermost ISO 8 Anteroom, the middle ISO 7 Buffer room, the 
innermost ISO 7 cleanroom, and its containing ISO 5 Biosafety Cabinets for CR115 and CR114 was 
performed on 26 Apr 2026. Cleaning was performed as per SOP 2.600.018 (Cleaning and Disinfecting 
Procedure for Microbiology). Following the monthly cleaning, it was documented that all H₂O₂ indicators 
passed. thus, confirming efficient monthly cleaning of all three parts of Cleanroom 115 and 114. 
 
Tables 2 & 3 (please see attached) present the environmental monitoring results for the duration of testing. 
The environmental monitoring (EM) plates were incubated for no less than 48 hours at 30-35°C and no 
less than an additional five days at 20-25°C as per SOP 2.600.002, Rev 15 (Environmental Monitoring of 
the Cleanroom Facility). Table 2 pertains to Environmental Monitoring performed during Celsis Sterility 
Processing, and Table 3 pertains to Environmental Monitoring performed during Celsis Sterility Aliquoting.

Environmental Monitoring from Celsis Sterility Processing: After reviewing the Environmental Monitoring results for the relevant testing dates, no 
microbial growth was detected on the personnel monitoring plates (analyst DS) or on the ISO 5 BSC (E001314) settling and surface plates for 
the date of testing (07May2026), the preceding date (06May2026), or the subsequent date (08May2026).
 
No growth was observed on weekly surface sampling plates for either the week prior to testing (29Apr2026) or the week of testing (08May2026). 
However, during the week prior to testing, 1 CFU (ETX-260506-0280) was detected on weekly active air sampling plates collected from the ISO 
8 area (Suite 115). Differntial staining confirmed the organism as Micrococcus luteus. During the week of testing (08May2026), 1 CFU (ETX-
260518-0249) was detected in the ISO 7 area (Suite 115A), identified as Staphylococcus aureus (Gram (+) cocci), along with 11 CFU (ETX-
260518-0250) in the ISO 7 area (Suite 115B), identified as Penicillium decumbens.
 
 
Environmental Monitoring from Celsis Sterility Aliquoting: After reviewing the Environmental Monitoring results for the relevant aliquoting and 
reading dates, no microbial growth was observed on ISO 5 BSC (E001798) surface and settling plates (analyst ALA) for the date of testing 
(14May2026), the preceding date (13May2026), or the subsequent date (18May2026). Additionally, no microbial growth was detected on active 
air sampling plates during the week prior to aliquoting (07May2026, analyst OA) or the week of testing (13May2026, analyst SMO).
 
However, 1 CFU (ETX-260518-0243) was isolated from floor surface sampling in the ISO 7 area of Suite 114A during the week prior to 
aliquoting (07May2026); the organism was identified as Bacillus subtilis (Gram (+) rods). No growth was observed on surface sampling plates 
during the week of testing.
 
Notably, the colony morphology of all microorganisms recovered from the processing cleanroom environments (Suite115B and Suite 114A) 
differed from that of the microorganism isolated from the test sample. This observation indicates that the environmental monitoring findings and 
the test sample contamination were likely isolated and unrelated events.
 
Also, the absence of contamination on analyst glove plates and work surface monitoring indicates that no viable transfer pathway existed from 
the ISO 8 area of Suite 114 to the ISO 5 BSC in Suite 114A where aliquoting was performed. Furthermore, the lack of contamination in other 
samples supports the fact that the testing environment was operating under optimal conditions. 
 
All analysts confirmed full compliance with cleaning procedures as outlined in SOPs 2.600.018 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for 
Microbiology), 2.600.059 (Celsis Sterility Testing), and 2.600.008 (USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test). A review of the available data confirms 
that the cleanroom and equipment conditions remained within acceptable parameters. No deviations or obvious signs of laboratory error during 
processing and aliquoting were noted from analysts. 
 
No other sample processed by analyst DS on 07May2026 and aliquoted by analyst ALA on 14May2026, failed Celsis Sterility testing that day.
 
An analysis of the sample history for Galleria Medical Pharmacy for last six months indicates that Eagle Analytical processed 339 total 
submissions (316 completed, 23 canceled) for Celsis Sterility testing, with six (ETX-260114-0138, ETX-260223-0247, ETX-260225-0451, ETX-
260225-0425, ETX-260305-0360, ETX-260327-0141) prior occurrence of a positive result recorded in the submission status during this period.
 
A review of the lot history shows that there have been no additional submissions of sample lot- 7501 for retesting using Celsis Sterility Testing 
or any other sterility method.
 
 
To assess the potential for sample-to-sample contamination as a contributing factor to the positive result, a comprehensive review was 
conducted of all samples processed during the same testing session on 07 May 2026. All other samples processed by analyst DS on the date of 
testing returned negative results with no evidence of microbial growth. The absence of positive findings across concurrently processed samples 
demonstrates that the testing environment was operating under controlled and acceptable conditions, and strongly indicates that cross-
contamination between samples did not occur. These findings support the conclusion that the positive result was an isolated event confined to 
the specific test article and was not attributable to analyst technique, sample handling procedures, or the laboratory environment.
 
 
A thorough review of all available data, encompassing reagents, supplies, cleanroom environmental monitoring, analyst performance records, 
and procedural compliance documentation, confirms that no contributing factors attributable to the laboratory or its personnel were identified. All 
critical control points were operating within established acceptance criteria at the time of testing and aliquoting. Accordingly, the positive result is 
considered a valid and isolated finding limited to the specific test article, with the probability of laboratory-introduced error effectively ruled out.
