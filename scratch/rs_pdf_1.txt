The analyst involved in the surface-sampling plate setup, Rey Estrada, and the analysts involved in 
reading the plate, Maraya Chukwumerije and Simin Mohammad, were interviewed comprehensively. 
Their responses are documented throughout this investigation.
 
The EM plates were stored in accordance with the supplier’s recommendations, visually inspected 
before use, and verified to be within their assigned expiration dates. All materials and supplies were 
disinfected in accordance with MICRO-SOP-9, Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology. 
The functionality of the incubators was verified through review of data generated by the in-house 
continuous monitoring system.
 
Surface sampling was performed by Rey Estrada in ISO 5 BSC E001313 located in room115A in suite 
CR115 on the date of testing (05 June 2026), in accordance with MICRO-SOP-2, Environmental 
Monitoring of the Cleanroom Facility.
 
The plates were initially incubated at a temperature of 30–35°C in incubator E001031 for a minimum 
duration of 48 hours, commencing on 05 June 2026. Following completion of the minimum 48 hours of 
incubation on 07 June 2026, no microbial growth was observed. The plates were subsequently 
incubated for a minimum of 5 days at 20–25°C in incubator E001034, with incubation ending on 15 
June 2026. At the completion of the second incubation period, 1 colony forming unit (CFU) was 
observed on Surface Plate #3. The plate was read by Maraya Chukwumerije after the initial incubation 
period and by Simin Mohammad after the second incubation period. Please see Table 1 for detailed 
information on the observations during the respective incubations. 
 
Based on the observations in Table 1, the surface-sampling plate recovery was submitted for microbial 
identification under ETX-260615-0435. The recovered microorganism was identified as Staphylococcus 
warneri.
 
To determine whether the organism was transient or recurring, personnel-monitoring plates for Rey 
Estrada and ISO 5 BSC E001313 environmental-monitoring plates were bracketed to include the date 
before testing (04 June 2026), the date of testing (05 June 2026), and the subsequent available 
monitoring date after testing (08 June 2026), as detailed in Table 2.

Environmental Monitoring Summary:
Personnel monitoring plates for Rey Estrada, including left- and right-touch plates, showed no microbial growth on the date 
before testing (04 June 2026) and the date of testing (05 June 2026). Personnel-monitoring data for the date after testing were 
not available because Rey Estrada resigned from his position before the scheduled follow-up monitoring could be performed.
 
For ISO 5 BSC E001313, daily surface sampling of four locations showed no microbial growth on the date before testing (04 
June 2026). On the date of testing (05 June 2026), 1 CFU was recovered from Surface #3 associated with Rey Estrada. The 
recovery was documented under ETX-260615-0435 and identified as Staphylococcus warneri. Surface sampling performed on 
the subsequent available monitoring date after testing (08 June 2026) showed no microbial growth.
 
Settling sampling of ISO 5 BSC E001313, including two locations, showed no microbial growth on the date before testing (04 
June 2026), the date of testing (05 June 2026), and the subsequent available monitoring date after testing (08 June 2026).
 
 
Weekly active-air monitoring of Suite 115 conducted during the week before testing (29 May 2026) showed no microbial growth. 
Weekly active-air monitoring conducted during the week of testing (05 June 2026) recovered 2 CFU in the ISO 8 area of Room 
115 under ETX-260615-0432. The organism was identified as Staphylococcus epidermidis.
 
Weekly surface monitoring of the anteroom and cleanroom areas associated with Suite 115 showed no microbial growth during 
the week before testing (29 May 2026) or the week of testing (05 June 2026).
 
During the interview, the analyst indicated that no obvious abnormalities or deviations occurred during the testing process. All 
materials were disinfected before testing, and the relevant cleanroom and ISO 5 BSC were cleaned and prepared before testing 
in accordance with MICRO SOP-2 and MICRO SOP-9.
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the cleanroom suite, including the ISO 8 anteroom (115), ISO 7 buffer room (115A), ISO 7 
cleanroom (115B), and the ISO 5 biosafety cabinets located within Room 115B, were performed on 26 April 2026 by Analyst - 
Rey Estrada - in accordance with MICRO SOP-9, Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology. All H₂O₂ indicators 
passed, confirming the successful completion and effectiveness of the monthly cleaning and disinfection activities within Rooms 
115, 115A, and 115B. Additionally, routine cleaning and disinfection were performed before and after the testing activity in 
accordance with MICRO SOP-9. 
 
It is also important to note that no samples processed by Rey Estrada in ISO 5 BSC E001313 on the date of testing (05 Jun 
2026) failed SCAN RDI testing.
 
Based on the available evidence, the recovery of 1 CFU of Staphylococcus warneri from Surface Plate #3 in ISO 5 BSC 
E001313 on the date of testing (05 June 2026) appears to be an isolated event. This assessment is supported by the absence of 
microbial recovery from Rey Estrada’s personnel-monitoring plates on the date before testing (04 June 2026) and the date of 
testing (05 June 2026); the absence of growth from BSC E001313 surface samples collected on the date before testing and the 
subsequent available monitoring date after testing (08 June 2026); and the absence of growth from ISO 5 settling plates 
throughout the bracketing period.
 
Although active-air monitoring recovered Staphylococcus epidermidis in the surrounding ISO 8 area of Room 115 during the 
week of testing, this recovery occurred outside the ISO 5 critical environment of BSC E001313. Staphylococcus epidermidis and 
Staphylococcus warneri are different organisms; therefore, the available data do not establish a direct microbiological 
relationship between the ISO 5 Surface Plate #3 recovery and the ISO 8 active-air recovery.
 
The negative ISO 5 settling, personnel, and bracketing surface monitoring results demonstrate that the BSC E001313 critical 
environment remained in a state of control. The available data do not support migration, persistence, or recurrence of 
contamination within ISO 5 BSC E001313. Collectively, the evidence supports that the recovery was an isolated, transient, and 
non-recurring event, while established cleaning, disinfection, and aseptic controls remained effective.
