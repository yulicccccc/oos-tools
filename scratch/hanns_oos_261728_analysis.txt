All analysts involved in the prepping, processing, and reading of the sample – Elysse Nioupin, Ayomide 
Odugbesi and Andrew Carrillo were interviewed comprehensively. Their answers are recorded 
throughout this document. 
  
Upon arrival, the sample was stored in accordance with the Client’s instructions. Analysts Elysse Nioupin 
and Ayomide Odugbesi verified the integrity of the sample throughout both the preparation and 
processing stages. No leaks or turbidity were observed at any point, verifying the integrity of the sample. 
  
All reagents and supplies mentioned in the material section above were stored according to the suppliers’ 
recommendations, and their integrity was visually verified before utilization. Moreover, all reagents and 
supplies had valid expiration dates. The functionality of all equipment was confirmed by reviewing data 
generated by our comprehensive in-house continuous monitoring system. 
  
The cleanroom suite (CR 115) used for processing procedures comprises three interconnected sections: 
the innermost ISO 7 cleanroom ( 115B), which connects to the middle ISO 7 buffer room (115A), and then 
to the outermost ISO 8 anteroom (115). A positive air pressure system is maintained throughout the suite 
to ensure controlled, unidirectional airflow from 115B through 115A and into 115. 
  
The ISO 5 BSC E001314, located in the innermost ISO 7 room, (115B), was used for sample processing 
steps. It was thoroughly cleaned and disinfected prior to each procedure in accordance with MICRO 
SOP-9 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology). Additionally, BSC E001314 was certified 
and approved by both the Engineering and Quality Assurance teams. Sample processing was conducted 
in the ISO 5 BSC E001314 in the innermost ISO 7 room, (115B) by Ayomide Odugbesi on 24 Jul 2026 as 
per MICRO-SOP-5 (USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test). 
  
On 24 Jul 2026, the sample vials for ETX-260722-0433 were received from the Sample Submissions 
team and brought into the Sterile Microbiology lab. Upon arrival, each sample vial was sprayed with an 
acidified bleach disinfectant, placed into pre-disinfected bins, and allowed a 10-minute contact time.

The secondary disinfection happened in the ISO 8 anteroom (115), where the vials were again treated 
with acidified bleach and provided a 10‑
 minutes contact time before processing. Subsequently, the vials 
were moved into innermost ISO 7 cleanroom (115B). Inside this cleanroom, the processing analyst, 
Ayomide Odugbesi, performed a final disinfection step, allowing an additional 10-minute contact time. 
Once fully disinfected, the vials were transferred into the ISO 5 BSC E001314. 
  
Inside the BSC, each container was aseptically opened, ensuring minimal exposure to the environment. 
The contents were then dispensed directly from the container into sterile canisters placed on a vacuum 
manifold for membrane filtration. The sample was processed and tested as per MICRO-SOP-5 (USP 
<71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test). Media bottles were transferred into incubators E001356 and E001357 to 
begin incubation. 
  
On 29 Jul 2026 (Day 5 of incubation), microbial growth was observed in TSB (Tryptic Soy Broth) media 
bottle by analyst, Andrew Carrillo, and confirmed by Microbiology Lab Supervisor, Robin Seymour. The 
positive TSB bottle for ETX-260722-0433 was submitted for Differential Staining and Microbial 
Identification under ETX-260729-0575. Differential staining indicated the presence of Gram (+) rods. 
Definitive identification identified one isolate as Bacillus litoralis; however, the second isolate could not be 
conclusively identified. 
  
  
The culture media utilized were within their expiry period. Negative control media bottles for the 
membrane filtration method were handled, processed, and incubated in the same manner as the test 
samples. No microbial growth was observed in the corresponding negative controls. 
  
Upon analyzing the environmental monitoring results, no microbial growth was observed in personnel 
sampling (left touch and right touch) plates for the prior date of testing, and date of testing. Also, surface 
sampling and settling plates for the date before testing, date of testing and date after testing showed no 
growth. From the day after testing, 1 CFU (ETX-260803-0463) was recovered from the personnel right 
touch plate and was identified as Micrococcus luteus (Gram (+) cocci). 
  
Weekly active air sampling identified low-level microbial recovery in both ISO 8 Room 115 and ISO 7 
Room 115B during the testing week and the week following testing. During the week of testing, 1 CFU 
(ETX-260803-0370) was recovered from ISO 8 Room 115 and differential staining characterized the 
isolate as Gram-positive cocci. In the same period, 1 CFU (ETX-260803-0373) was recovered from ISO 7 
Room 115B and identified as Micrococcus luteus (Gram-positive cocci). During the week following 
testing, 3 CFU (ETX-260811-0455) exhibiting Gram-positive cocci morphology were recovered from ISO 
8 Room 115. Additionally, 1 CFU (ETX-260811-0456) was recovered from ISO 7 Room 115B and 
identified as Kocuria indica, Kocuria rhizophila/sp. BT304, and Micrococcus luteus, all of which are 
Gram-positive cocci. Also during the week following testing, 1 CFU (ETX-260811-0452) was recovered 
from the floor of Room 115A and identified as Brevibacterium frigoritolerans (Gram-positive rod).

Although microbial growth was recovered from the personnel monitoring (right glove touch plate), it is important 
to note that this isolate was detected on the subsequent testing date and therefore could not have impacted 
sample processed prior to its recovery.  
 
Furthermore, the morphology of this isolate differed from that of the organism recovered from the test sample. 
In addition, Brevibacterium frigoritolerans was detected on the cleanroom floor (115A) and exhibited 
morphological similarity to the organism isolated from the test sample. However, this organism was recovered 
the week after testing in the cleanroom 115A while testing was done in cleanroom 115B the week prior. 
Moreover, test samples do not come into direct contact with floor surfaces. Samples are prepared and placed 
in disinfected, lidded bins and transported on a cart within the ISO 7 cleanroom prior to testing. 
  
Importantly, the absence of contamination on analyst glove plates and work-surface environmental monitoring 
plates collected on the date of testing indicates that there were no viable transfer of contaminants to the ISO 5 
biological safety cabinet (BSC). Additionally, the lack of contamination in other samples processed during the 
same testing session further demonstrates that the testing environment was operating in optimal condition. 
  
Therefore, considering that no viable organisms were recovered from the ISO 5 environment, personnel 
monitoring results were acceptable on the date of testing, and the environmental isolates were not recovered 
from locations or surfaces directly involved in sample preparation or testing, the environmental isolates are not 
considered a credible source of the contamination detected in the test sample. 
  
The analysts confirmed full compliance with cleaning procedures as outlined in MICRO SOP-9 (Cleaning and 
Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology) and MICRO-SOP-5 (USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test). 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the outermost ISO 8 Anteroom, the middle ISO 7 Buffer room, the 
innermost ISO 7 cleanroom, and its containing ISO 5 Biosafety Cabinets for Cleanroom suite CR115 was 
performed on 30 Jun 2026, as per MICRO-SOP-9 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology). 
During both cleaning cycles, it was documented that all H₂O₂ indicators passed. This confirms the efficient 
monthly cleaning of all three parts of Cleanroom suite CR115. 
  
Analyzing a 6-month sample history for Hann's Pharmacy (E71710), this specific analyte 
"C-Tirzepatide-Pyridoxine 10mg-2mg-1ml " has had no prior failures using USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test 
during this period. 
  
To assess the potential for sample-to-sample contamination contributing to the positive results, a 
comprehensive review was conducted of all samples processed on the same day. All other samples processed 
by the analyst and other analysts that day tested negative. These findings suggest that cross-contamination 
between samples is highly unlikely. 
  
Based on the observations outlined above, it is unlikely that the failing results were due to reagents, supplies, 
the cleanroom environment, the process, or analyst involvement. Consequently, the possibility of laboratory 
error contributing to this failure is minimal and the original result is deemed to be valid.
