Purpose:
This procedure describes the requirements, methods, and documentation of nonviable 
particles and viable microbes monitored in the cleanroom facility at Eagle. 
2.0

This procedure is applicable to the Anteroom (ISO 8), Cleanroom (ISO 7), and Biological 
Safety Cabinets (ISO 5) at Eagle.

4.0
Definitions:
4.1
Alert Level: A level of microorganisms that shows a potential drift from normal 
operating conditions and is based on historical data. 
4.2
Action Level: A higher level of contamination than the alert level that, if exceeded, 
is answered by an immediate follow-up, directed toward investigating the source of 
contamination and controlling it, resulting in a Corrective Action Response (CAR).
4.3
Anteroom: An ISO 8, or better area where personnel garbing, staging of components, 
and other designated activities are performed.

4.4
Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC): a ventilated cabinet for compounded sterile 
preparations, personnel, product, and environmental protection having an open front 
with inward airflow for personnel protection, downward high-efficiency particulate 
air (HEPA)-filtered laminar airflow for product protection, and HEPA-filtered 
exhausted air for environmental protection. 
4.5
Clean Room/Buffer Area: An ISO 7 room in which the concentration of airborne 
particles is controlled to meet a specified airborne particulate cleanliness class. 
Microorganisms in the environment are monitored so that a microbial level for air, 
surface, and personnel gear is not exceeded for the specified cleanliness class.
4.6
Clean Room Facility: The controlled area consists of an anteroom, cleanrooms, and 
BSCs.
4.7
Contact Plate/Replicate Organisms Detection and Counting (RODAC): nutrient agar 
plate containing Lecithin and Polysorbate 80 that is used for the detection and 
enumeration of microorganisms present on surfaces. 
4.8
Colony Forming Unit (CFU): a unit used to estimate the number of viable bacteria or 
fungal cells in a sample.
4.9
International Organization for Standardization (ISO): The International Organization 
for Standardization is an international standard-setting body composed of 
representatives from various national standards organizations.
4.10 United States Pharmacopeia National Formulary (USP) – The USP-NF contains the 
pharmacopeia standards for medicines, dosage forms, drug substances, excipients, 
medical devices, and dietary supplements.
4.11 Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) – Controlled Document with a unique 
numerical identifier that provides instructions regarding a particular operational 
procedure.
4.12 Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA): a non-selective culture medium used for the isolation and 
cultivation of microorganisms. 
4.13 Sabouraud Dextrose Media (SDA): selective media primarily used for the isolation 
of fungi, dermatophytes, and yeasts.
4.14 Testing Process/Session: A Testing Session is defined as the complete and continuous 
period during which a sample or group of samples is subjected to a specific testing 
method. This session encompasses all procedures, equipment, personnel activities, 
and environmental conditions directly associated with the execution of that method. 
All preparatory, in-process, and post-process steps that are integral to the testing 
method are considered part of the testing process. For example, in the case of USP 
<71> Sterility Testing, the testing session includes all activities performed within the 
cleanroom, including the use and monitoring of the Biosafety Cabinet (BSC), as these 
are essential components of the sterility testing process.

8.3
Action and Alert Investigations for Environmental Sampling
8.3.1
All microbial growth observed from environmental monitoring results shall 
be sent out for differential staining (if feasible) and identification to the 
species level.
8.3.1.1
Isolates found within the ISO 8 only need to be stained as per SOP 
2.600.020, Differential Staining, except in the following 
circumstances
8.3.1.1.1
Submit any isolates recovered from the ISO 8 area to the 
R&D Microbiology Lab for microbial identification, 
provided they are linked to samples that produced out-
of-specification results during the same testing session. 
8.3.1.1.2
Submit ISO 8 isolates if CFU is at or above the Action 
Level
Note: All fungal isolates recovered, regardless of ISO classification, must 
be identified to ensure appropriate documentation, risk assessment, and 
compliance with microbiological standards.
8.3.2
If the microbial contamination is greater than the designated action limit, 
follow SOP 2.100.019, Out-of-Specification (OOS) – Test Failure 
Investigation, to initiate an OOS investigation.
8.3.2.1
If a Sterility Test OOS occurs on the same day as the environmental 
monitoring OOS, a thorough investigation of the cleanroom 
facility, personnel, and other microbiological processes shall be 
initiated.
8.4
Action and Alert Investigations for Physical or Operational Parameters of the 
Anteroom and Buffer Room
8.4.1
Physical or operational parameters include relative humidity, temperature, 
and pressure of the cleanroom facility. These parameters are monitored 
electronically through the Lighthouse System. The action and alert levels are 
defined as such in SOP 2.700.014 Use of Lighthouse for Monitoring 
Environmental Conditions for the Houston location, and in SOP 2.700.059 
Use of Aircare Automation System for monitoring environmental conditions 
for the Canada Location.
