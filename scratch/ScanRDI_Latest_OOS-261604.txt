All analysts involved in the preparation, processing, and reading of the samples Elysse Nioupin, Varsha Subramanian, Ellen 
Bennett, and Gerald Anyangwe were interviewed, and their responses are documented throughout this report.
 
The samples were stored upon arrival in accordance with the Client’s instructions. Elysse Nioupin and Varsha Subramanian 
confirmed sample integrity throughout both the preparation and processing stages. No leaks or turbidity were observed at any point
, confirming the integrity of the samples.
 
All reagents and supplies listed in the materials section were stored in accordance with the supplier’s recommendations, and their 
integrity was visually verified prior to use. In addition, all reagents and supplies were within their established expiration dates at the 
time of use.
 
During the preparation phase, Elysse Nioupin disinfected the samples using acidified bleach and transferred them into a pre-
disinfected storage bin. On 10 Jul 2026, prior to sample processing, Varsha Subramanian performed a second disinfection with 
acidified bleach and allowed a minimum contact time of 10 minutes before the samples were transferred into the controlled 
cleanroom suite. 
 
The samples entered through an outermost ISO 8 cleanroom (CR142) and progressed into buffer ISO 8 cleanroom (CR143) before 
being introduced into the adjoining ISO 7 cleanroom (CR144) and finally into innermost ISO 7 cleanroom (CR145). Together, these 
rooms form the L-suite, so named for their L-shaped configuration. Final disinfection was completed immediately prior to placement 
into the ISO 5 Biological Safety Cabinet, E001317, located in CR145. All activities were performed in accordance with SOP 
2.600.023, Rapid Scan RDI® Test Using FIFU Method.
 
 
The ISO 7 cleanroom (CR145) is configured as a controlled cleanroom suite utilized for pre-labeling and filtration activities. The 
room is divided into two sections (Section I and Section II), which collectively support aseptic processing . Adjacent CR144 is a 
separate ISO 7 controlled area that is similarly divided into two designated sections (Section I and Section II).  CR143 serves as 
the ISO 8 buffer room, while CR142 functions as the ISO 8 anteroom providing controlled access to the suite. A positive pressure 
cascade is maintained throughout the interconnected areas to facilitate directional airflow from higher- to lower-classified spaces, 
thereby supporting effective contamination control and maintaining the required environmental conditions within all classified areas.
 
 
The ISO 5 BSC E001317, located in CR145, was used for the pre-labelling and filtration steps, while the ISO 5 BSC E001937, 
located in CR144, was used for changeover steps. Both units were thoroughly cleaned and disinfected prior to each procedure in 
accordance with SOP 2.600.018, Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology. In addition, both BSCs were certified and 
approved by Engineering and Quality Assurance. Sample processing was conducted by Varsha Subramanian in CR145, and 
changeover was conducted by Ellen Bennett in CR144 on 10 Jul 2026.
 
Gerald Anyangwe confirmed that equipment E001040 was set up in accordance with SOP 2.700.004, Scan RDI® System – 
Operations (Standard C3 Quality Check and Microscope Setup and Maintenance), and that both the negative control and positive 
control yielded the expected results.

JEsqueda , 9/30/2025, 4:15:53 PM
On 10 Jul 2026, a rapid sterility test was performed on the sample using the ScanRDI method. Sample 
processing was conducted by Analyst Varsha Subramanian, and the final reading was carried out by Gerald 
Anyangwe. The test detected 128 total events, of which 9 were confirmed as viable microbial events exhibiting 
rod-shaped morphology (see Table 1).
 
Table 2 and Table 3 present the environmental monitoring results for sample ETX-260706-0599. 
Environmental monitoring (EM) plates were incubated for a minimum of 48 hours at 30–35°C, followed by no 
less than five additional days at 20–25°C, in accordance with SOP 2.600.002 (Environmental Monitoring of the 
Cleanroom Facility).
 
Review of the environmental monitoring data showed no microbial growth in personnel monitoring samples (left 
and right touch), ISO 5 surface sampling, or ISO 5 settling plates associated with both the processing analyst 
VV (Biological Safety Cabinet E001317) and the changeover analyst ELB (Biological Safety Cabinet E001937).
 
However, the following findings were noted:
 
One CFU (ETX-260720-0292) was recovered during weekly active air sampling in the ISO 7 area bracketing 
the suite (Room 145); the organisms were identified as Gram-positive and Gram-negative short rods.
 
Seventeen CFUs (ETX-260720-0295) were recovered during weekly active air sampling in Section I of the ISO 
8 area (Room 143). The organisms were identified as Gram-positive rods and are currently under investigation 
as OOS # 261657.
 
Eleven CFUs (ETX-260720-0299) were recovered during weekly active air sampling in Section II of the ISO 8 
area (Room 143); the organisms were identified as Gram-positive cocci and are currently under investigation 
as OOS # 261657.
 
Seven CFUs (ETX-260720-0302) were recovered during weekly active air sampling in the ISO 8 area (Room 
142); the organisms were identified as Gram-positive cocci.
 
Weekly surface sampling of the anteroom and cleanroom bracketing the suite showed no microbial growth.
 
The microbial recoveries identified during environmental monitoring are not considered to have impacted the 
sterility test results. All product-contact activities, including processing and changeover, were performed within 
certified ISO 5 biological safety cabinets (BSC E001317 and BSC E001937), which provide a significantly 
higher level of environmental control than the ISO 7 and ISO 8 areas where the air recoveries were detected. 
Environmental monitoring performed on the date of testing demonstrated no microbial growth in personnel 
monitoring samples, ISO 5 surface samples, or ISO 5 settling plates associated with either processing or 
changeover activities. In addition, the cleanroom suites operate under a positive-pressure airflow design that 
directs air from the cleaner ISO 5/ISO 7 areas outward toward the ISO 7/ISO 8 anterooms, minimizing the 
potential for contamination migration from the lower-classification areas into the critical processing 
environments.

JEsqueda , 9/30/2025, 4:15:53 PM
Furthermore, the organisms recovered from the ISO 8 active air samples (Section II and Room 142) were 
identified as Gram-positive cocci, whereas the microorganisms detected in the ScanRDI test sample 
exhibited rod-shaped morphology. This morphological distinction indicates that at least a portion of the 
environmental monitoring recoveries were not consistent with the microorganisms detected in the sample.
Although rod-shaped organisms were recovered during environmental monitoring, these findings were 
limited to a single CFU recovered from the ISO 7 (CR145) and seventeen CFUs recovered from ISO 8 
(CR143) Section I. Importantly, these recoveries were confined to the ambient air of the surrounding lower-
classification areas and were not associated with the ISO 5 processing environment. In addition, no 
corresponding microbial growth was detected on personnel monitoring samples, ISO 5 surface samples, or 
settling plates. When considered in conjunction with the satisfactory cleaning and disinfection activities 
performed prior to processing, the absence of contamination in critical environmental monitoring locations, 
and the negative results obtained for all other samples processed on the same day, the available evidence 
supports the conclusion that the microbial growth detected in the ISO 7 and ISO 8 areas neither 
contributed to nor affected the positive ScanRDI result.
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the ISO 7 cleanroom and associated ISO 5 biosafety cabinets (BSCs) 
were performed on 14 Jun 2026, using hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), in accordance with SOP 2.600.018 
(Cleaning and Disinfection Procedure). All H₂O₂ indicators met acceptance criteria.
 
A six-month historical review of samples from Tailor made compounding identified one prior out-of-
specification (OOS-260213) result for the same product, Semaglutide/Glycine 2.5mg/5mg/mL (2mL), using 
the ScanRDI method.
 
To evaluate the potential for sample-to-sample contamination, all samples processed on the same day 
were reviewed. All other samples processed by the same analyst and by other analysts on that day yielded 
negative results, indicating that cross-contamination is unlikely.
 
Based on the cumulative evidence, it is highly unlikely that the failing results were due to reagents, 
supplies, the cleanroom environment, the process, or analyst involvement. Consequently, the possibility of 
laboratory error contributing to this failure is minimal. Therefore, the original test result is deemed valid.
