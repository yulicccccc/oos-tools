The analyst involved in the surface sampling plate setup, Clea S. Garza (CGS), and the analysts 
involved in reading the plate, Maraya Chukwumerije and Sophia Santamaria, were interviewed 
comprehensively. Their responses are documented throughout this investigation.
 
The EM plates were stored in accordance with the supplier's recommendations, visually inspected 
before use, and verified to be within their assigned expiration dates. All materials and supplies were 
disinfected in accordance with MICRO-SOP-9, Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for 
Microbiology. The functionality of the incubators was verified through review of data generated by 
the in-house continuous monitoring system.
 
Surface Sampling was performed by analyst Clea S. Garza during the ScanRDI changeover 
process in ISO 5 BSC E001309 located in cleanroom suite CR117A on 11 May 2026, in accordance 
with MICRO-SOP-2, Environmental Monitoring of the Cleanroom Facility.
 
The plates were initially incubated at a temperature of 30-35°C in incubator E001031 for a minimum 
duration of 48 hours, commencing on 11 May 2026. Following completion of the minimum 48 hours 
of incubation on 13 May 2026, no microbial growth was observed. The plates were subsequently 
incubated for a minimum of 5 days at 20-25°C in incubator E001034, with incubation ending on 18 
May 2026. Post-completion of the second incubation period, one colony-like artifact was observed 
on Suface Sampling Plate# 1. Please see Table 1 for detailed information on the observations 
during the respective incubations.
 
The observed artifact was submitted for microbial identification under ETX-260518-0273. However, 
following transfer or inoculation onto fresh media, no growth was observed. Therefore, the observed 
artifact could not be confirmed as a viable microbial colony, and hence, could not be reported as a 
confirmed CFU. The lack of growth upon subculture indicates that the observation may have been 
non-viable material. 
 
To evaluate environmental controls and determine whether the recovery was transient or recurring, 
personnel-monitoring plates for Clea S. Garza and ISO 5 BSC E001309 environmental-monitoring 
plates were bracketed across the testing period, as detailed in Table 2.

Upon analyzing the environmental monitoring results summarized in Table 2, personnel 
monitoring plates for Clea S. Garza, including left- and right-touch plates, showed no 
microbial growth on the date before testing (08 May 2026), the date of testing (11 May 2026), 
and the date after testing (12 May 2026).
 
For ISO 5 BSC E001309, daily surface sampling of four locations showed no microbial growth 
on the date before testing (09 May 2026). On the date of testing (11 May 2026), 1 CFU was 
recovered from Surface sampling plate# 1 associated with Clea S. Garza. The recovery was 
documented under ETX-260518-0273; microbial identification indicated a colony-like artifact 
(White, shiny, hardened, smooth area embeded on agar), however, no microbial growth could 
be recovered from the subculture. Surface sampling performed on the date after testing (12 
May 2026) showed no microbial growth across all locations.
 
Settling sampling of ISO 5 BSC E001309, showed no microbial growth on the date before 
testing (09 May 2026), the date of testing (11 May 2026), and the date after testing (12 May 
2026).
 
Weekly active-air monitoring of Suite 117 conducted during the week of testing (07 May 2026) 
recovered 2 CFUs in ISO 8 CR117 under ETX-260518-0263, which were morpholgically 
identified as Gram-positive cocci and Gram-positive pleomorphic rods. Weekly surface 
monitoring of the anteroom and cleanroom areas associated with Suite 117 showed no 
microbial growth on during the week of testing on 07 May 2026.
 
During the interview, the analyst indicated that no obvious abnormalities or deviations 
occurred during the testing process. All materials were disinfected before testing, and the 
relevant cleanroom and ISO 5 BSC were cleaned and prepared before testing in accordance 
with MICRO-SOP-2 and MICRO-SOP-9.
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the cleanroom suite, including the ISO 8 anteroom 
(Room 117) and ISO 7 buffer room (Room 117A) housing ISO 5 BSC E001309, were 
performed on 26-Apr-2026 by analysts - Tamiru Kotisso and Cuong Du - in accordance with 
MICRO-SOP-9, Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology. All H2O2 indicators 
passed, confirming the successful completion and effectiveness of the monthly cleaning and 
disinfection activities within Suite 117. Additionally, routine cleaning and disinfection were 
performed before and after the testing activity in accordance with MICRO-SOP-9.
 
It is also important to note that no product samples processed by Clea S. Garza in ISO 5 BSC 
E001309 on the date of testing (11 May 2026) failed ScanRDI testing.

[Text Field51]: "Based on the available evidence, one colony-like artifact was observed on ISO 5 BSC E001309 Surface Samplinf Plate# 1 on the date of testing (11 May 2026). However, the observation could not be confirmed as a viable microbial CFU because no growth was obtained following inoculation onto fresh media. Therefore, the observation represents a nonviable or non-microbial artifact.\r \rEven if the observed artifact had represented a viable CFU, it would appear to be an isolated event. This assessment is supported by the absence of microbial recovery from Clea S. Garza's personnel-monitoring plates on the date before testing (08 May 2026), the date of testing (11 May 2026), and the date after testing (12 May 2026); the absence of growth from BSC E001309 surface samples collected on the date before testing (09 May 2026) and the date after testing (12 May 2026); and the absence of growth from ISO 5 settling plates throughout the bracketing period. Additionally, it is important to note that no growth was observed on the other three surface sampling plates from the date of testing.\r \rThe negative ISO 5 settling, personnel, and bracketing surface monitoring results demonstrate that the BSC E001309 critical environment remained in a state of control. The available data do not support migration, persistence, or recurrence of contamination within ISO 5 BSC E001309. Collectively, the evidence supports that the recovery was an isolated, transient, and non-recurring event and could likely be attributed to an inadvertent laboratory error.\r \rAccordingly, no systemic environmental control deficiencies were identified, and no additional corrective or preventive actions are warranted at this time beyond continued routine environmental monitoring and adherence to approved cleaning, disinfection, and aseptic procedures."

Based on the available evidence, one colony-like artifact was observed on ISO 5 BSC 
E001309 Surface Samplinf Plate# 1 on the date of testing (11 May 2026). However, the 
observation could not be confirmed as a viable microbial CFU because no growth was 
obtained following inoculation onto fresh media. Therefore, the observation represents a 
nonviable or non-microbial artifact.
 
Even if the observed artifact had represented a viable CFU, it would appear to be an isolated 
event. This assessment is supported by the absence of microbial recovery from Clea S. 
Garza's personnel-monitoring plates on the date before testing (08 May 2026), the date of 
testing (11 May 2026), and the date after testing (12 May 2026); the absence of growth from 
BSC E001309 surface samples collected on the date before testing (09 May 2026) and the 
date after testing (12 May 2026); and the absence of growth from ISO 5 settling plates 
throughout the bracketing period. Additionally, it is important to note that no growth was 
observed on the other three surface sampling plates from the date of testing.
 
The negative ISO 5 settling, personnel, and bracketing surface monitoring results 
demonstrate that the BSC E001309 critical environment remained in a state of control. The 
available data do not support migration, persistence, or recurrence of contamination within 
ISO 5 BSC E001309. Collectively, the evidence supports that the recovery was an isolated, 
transient, and non-recurring event and could likely be attributed to an inadvertent laboratory 
error.
 
Accordingly, no systemic environmental control deficiencies were identified, and no additional 
corrective or preventive actions are warranted at this time beyond continued routine 
environmental monitoring and adherence to approved cleaning, disinfection, and aseptic 
procedures.
