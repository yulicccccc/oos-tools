plate, Maraya Chukwumerije (MC) and Simin Mohammad (SMO), were interviewed comprehensively. Their responses are 
documented throughout this investigation.
 
The EM plates were stored in accordance with the supplier’s recommendations, visually inspected before use, and verified to 
be within their assigned expiration dates. All materials and supplies were disinfected in accordance with MICRO-SOP-9, 
Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology. The functionality of the incubators was verified through review of data 
generated by the in-house continuous monitoring system.
 
Surface sampling was performed by Guanchen (David) Li in ISO 5 BSC E001314 located in room 115B in suite CR115 on 
the date of testing (04 June 2026), in accordance with MICRO-SOP-2, Environmental Monitoring of the Cleanroom Facility.
 
The plates were initially incubated at a temperature of 30–35°C in incubator E001031 for a minimum duration of 48 hours, 
commencing on 04 June 2026. Following completion of the minimum 48 hours of incubation on 06 June 2026, no microbial 
growth was observed. The plates were subsequently incubated for a minimum of 5 days at 20–25°C in incubator E001034, 
with incubation ending on 11 June 2026. At completion of the second incubation period, one colony-like artifact was observed 
on Surface Plate #1.
 
The observed artifact was submitted for microbial identification under ETX-260615-0424. However, following transfer or 
inoculation onto fresh media, no growth was observed. Therefore, the observed artifact could not be confirmed as a viable 
microbial colony or reported as a confirmed colony forming unit (CFU). The lack of growth upon subculture indicates that the 
observation may have been nonviable material, an artifact associated with agar preparation or pouring, or another non-
microbial artifact.
 
 
The plate was read by Maraya Chukwumerije after the initial incubation period and by Simin Mohammad after the second 
incubation period. Please see Table 1 for detailed information on the observations during the respective incubations.
 
To determine whether the recovery was transient or recurring, personnel-monitoring plates for Guanchen (David) Li and ISO 
5 BSC E001314 environmental-monitoring plates were bracketed to include the date before testing (03 June 2026), the date

Environmental Monitoring Summary: Personnel monitoring plates for Guanchen (David) Li, including left- and right-touch plates, 
showed no microbial growth on the date before testing (03 June 2026), the date of testing (04 June 2026), and the date after testing (05 
June 2026).
 
For ISO 5 BSC E001314, daily surface sampling of four locations showed no microbial growth on the date before testing (03 June 
2026). On the date of testing (04 June 2026), 1 CFU was recovered from Surface #1 associated with Guanchen (David) Li. The 
recovery was documented under ETX-260615-0424; microbial identification was not available at the time of review. Surface sampling 
performed on the date after testing (05 June 2026) showed no microbial growth.
 
Settling sampling of ISO 5 BSC E001314, including two locations, showed no microbial growth on the date before testing (03 June 
2026), the date of testing (04 June 2026), and the date after testing (05 June 2026).
 
Weekly active-air monitoring of Suite 115 conducted during the week before testing (29 May 2026) showed no microbial growth. Weekly 
active-air monitoring conducted during the week after testing (05 June 2026) recovered 2 CFU in the ISO 8 area of Room 115 under 
ETX-260615-0432. The organism was identified as Staphylococcus epidermidis.
 
Weekly surface monitoring of the anteroom and cleanroom areas associated with Suite 115 showed no microbial growth during the 
week before testing (29 May 2026) or the week after testing (05 June 2026).
 
During the interview, the analyst indicated that no obvious abnormalities or deviations occurred during the testing process. All materials 
were disinfected before testing, and the relevant cleanroom and ISO 5 BSC were cleaned and prepared before testing in accordance 
with MICRO-SOP-2 and MICRO-SOP-9.
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the cleanroom suite, including the ISO 8 anteroom (115), ISO 7 buffer room (115A), ISO 7 
cleanroom (115B), and the ISO 5 biosafety cabinets located within Room 115B, were performed on 26 April 2026 by Analyst - Rey 
Estrada - in accordance with MICRO SOP-9, Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology. All H₂O₂ indicators passed, 
confirming the successful completion and effectiveness of the monthly cleaning and disinfection activities within Rooms 115, 115A, and 
115B. Additionally, routine cleaning and disinfection were performed before and after the testing activity in accordance with MICRO 
SOP-9.
 
It is also important to note that no samples processed by Guanchen(David) Li in ISO 5 BSC E001314 on the date of testing (04 Jun 
2026) failed sterility testing.
 
 
Based on the available evidence, one colony-like artifact was observed on Surface Plate #1 in ISO 5 BSC E001314 on the date of 
testing (04 June 2026). However, the observation could not be confirmed as a viable microbial CFU because no growth was obtained 
following inoculation onto fresh media. Therefore, the observation may represent a nonviable or non-microbial artifact, including a 
potential artifact associated with agar preparation or the agar-pouring process.
 
If the observed artifact had represented a viable CFU, it would appear to be an isolated event. This assessment is supported by the 
absence of microbial recovery from Guanchen (David) Li’s personnel-monitoring plates on the date before testing (03 June 2026), the 
date of testing (04 June 2026), and the date after testing (05 June 2026); the absence of growth from BSC E001314 surface samples 
collected on the date before testing and the date after testing; and the absence of growth from ISO 5 settling plates throughout the 
bracketing period.
 
Although active-air monitoring recovered Staphylococcus epidermidis in the surrounding ISO 8 area of Room 115 during the week after 
testing (05 June 2026), this recovery occurred outside the ISO 5 critical environment of BSC E001314. In addition, microbial 
identification of the ISO 5 Surface # 1 recovery was not available at the time of review. Therefore, a microbiological relationship 
between the ISO 5 surface recovery and the ISO 8 active-air recovery cannot be established. 
 
The negative ISO 5 settling, personnel, and bracketing surface monitoring results demonstrate that the BSC E001314 critical 
environment remained in a state of control. The available data do not support migration, persistence, or recurrence of contamination 
within ISO 5 BSC E001314. Collectively, the evidence supports that the recovery was an isolated, transient, and non-recurring event, 
while established cleaning, disinfection, and aseptic controls remained effective.
