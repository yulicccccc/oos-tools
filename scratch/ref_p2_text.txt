All the analysts involved in the testing - the initial processing analyst (Guanchen Li), the processing analyst who

INITIAL TEST UNDER SUBMISSION ETX-260811-0261
All analysts involved in the prepping, processing, and reading of the sample – Andrew Carrillo, Guanchen Li and Elysse 
Nioupin - were interviewed comprehensively. Their answers are recorded throughout this document.
 
Upon arrival, the sample was stored in accordance with the Client’s instructions. Analysts - Andrew Carrillo and 
Guanchen Li - verified the integrity of the sample throughout both the preparation and processing stages. No leaks or 
turbidity were observed at any point, verifying the integrity of the sample.
 
All reagents and supplies mentioned in the material section above were stored according to the suppliers’ 
recommendations, and their integrity was visually verified before utilization. Moreover, all reagents and supplies had 
valid expiration dates. The functionality of all equipment was confirmed by reviewing data generated by our 
comprehensive in-house continuous monitoring system.
 
The cleanroom suite (CR 115) used for processing procedures comprises three interconnected sections: the innermost 
ISO 7 cleanroom (115B), which connects to the middle ISO 7 buffer room (115A), and then to the outermost ISO 8 
anteroom (115). A positive air pressure system is maintained throughout the suite to ensure controlled, unidirectional 
airflow from 115B through 115A and into 115.
 
The ISO 5 Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC) E001314, located in the innermost ISO 7 room, (115B), was used for sample 
processing steps. It was thoroughly cleaned and disinfected prior to each procedure in accordance with MICRO SOP-9 
(Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology). Additionally, BSC E001314 was certified and approved by both 
the Engineering and Quality Assurance teams. Sample processing was conducted in the ISO 5 BSC E001314 in the 
innermost ISO 7 room, (115B) by Guanchen Li on 20 Aug 2026 as per MICRO-SOP-5 (USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility 
Test).

On 20 Aug 2026, the sample vials for ETX-260811-0261 were received from the Sample Submissions team and brought into the Sterile 
Microbiology lab. Upon arrival, each sample vial was sprayed with an acidified bleach disinfectant, placed into pre-disinfected bins, and allowed a 
10-minute contact time.
The secondary disinfection happened in the ISO 8 anteroom (115), where the vials were again treated with acidified bleach and provided a 10‑ 
minutes contact time before processing. Subsequently, the vials were moved into innermost ISO 7 cleanroom (115B). Inside this cleanroom, the 
processing analyst, Guanchen Li, performed a final disinfection step, allowing an additional 10-minute contact time. Once fully disinfected, the vials 
were transferred into the ISO 5 BSC E001314.
 
Inside the BSC, each container was aseptically opened, ensuring minimal exposure to the environment. The contents were then dispensed directly 
from the container into sterile canisters placed on a vacuum manifold for membrane filtration. The sample was processed and tested as per 
MICRO-SOP-5 (USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test). Media bottles were transferred into incubators E001356 and E001357 to begin incubation.
On 27 Aug 2026 (Day 7 of incubation), microbial growth was observed in one TSB (Tryptic Soy Broth) media bottle by analyst, Elysse Nioupin, and 
confirmed by Microbiology Lab Supervisor, Robin Seymour. The positive TSB bottle for ETX-260811-0261 was submitted for Differential Staining 
and Microbial Identification under ETX-260827-0547. Differential staining indicated the presence of Gram-positive rods. Definitive identification 
identified the isolate as Bacillus velezensis (Gram-positive rods).
 
The culture media utilized were within their expiry period. Negative control media bottles for the membrane filtration method were handled, 
processed, and incubated in the same manner as the test samples. No microbial growth was observed in the corresponding negative controls.
Upon analyzing the environmental monitoring results, no microbial growth was detected on personnel monitoring plates, settling plates, or ISO 5 
BSC surface sampling plates on the date of testing, the preceding testing date, or the subsequent testing date. Additionally, no microbial growth 
was recovered from the weekly surface sampling plate collected on 20 Aug 2026. However, weekly active air monitoring of ISO 8 Room 115 (CR 
115) recovered 9 CFUs (ETX-260831-0366). Differential staining confirmed the organisms to be Gram-positive cocci.
 
It is important to note that the colony morphology of the microorganisms recovered from the cleanroom environment differed from that of the 
microorganism isolated from the test sample. This morphological distinction indicates that the environmental monitoring recovery and the test 
sample contamination were isolated and unrelated events.
 
In addition, considering the facility's layered disinfection program, controlled unidirectional airflow, and cleanroom design, it is unlikely that the 
microbial contaminant detected in the ISO 8 environment impacted on the sample processed within the ISO 5 area of the ISO 7 cleanroom (Suite 
115B).
 
The absence of contamination on analyst glove plates and work-surface environmental monitoring plates collected on the date of testing indicates 
that there were no viable transfer of contaminants to the ISO 5 biological safety cabinet (BSC). Additionally, the lack of contamination in other 
samples processed during the same testing session further demonstrates that the testing environment was operating in optimal condition.
 
Therefore, considering that no viable organisms were recovered from the ISO 5 environment, personnel monitoring results were acceptable on the 
date of testing, and the environmental isolates were not recovered from locations or surfaces directly involved in sample preparation or testing, the 
environmental isolates are not considered a credible source of the contamination detected in the test sample.
 
The analysts confirmed full compliance with cleaning procedures as outlined in MICRO SOP-9 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for 
Microbiology) and MICRO-SOP-5 (USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test).
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the outermost ISO 8 Anteroom, the middle ISO 7 Buffer room, the innermost ISO 7 cleanroom, and its 
containing ISO 5 BSCs for Cleanroom suite CR115 was performed on 30 Jun 2026, as per MICRO-SOP-9 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure 
for Microbiology). During both cleaning cycles, it was documented that all H₂O₂ indicators passed. This confirms the efficient monthly cleaning of 
all three parts of Cleanroom suite CR115.
 
Analyzing a 6-month sample history for Uriel Pharmacy (E02836), this specific analyte " Viscum Album 20mg " has had no prior failures using USP 
<71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test during this period.
 
To assess the potential for sample-to-sample contamination contributing to the positive results, a comprehensive review was conducted of all 
samples processed on the same day. All other samples processed by the analyst and other analysts that day tested negative. These findings 
suggest that cross-contamination between samples is highly unlikely.
 
Based on the observations outlined above, it is unlikely that the failing results were due to reagents, supplies, the cleanroom environment, the 
process, or analyst involvement. Consequently, the possibility of laboratory error contributing to this failure is minimal and the original result is 
deemed to be valid.

RETEST UNDER SUBMISSION ETX-260831-0275.
A retest was performed under ETX-260831-0275, as the client submitted additional sample 
vials from  the same lot for retesting, at their discretion, without prior notification or 
authorization from the laboratory.
 
Analogous to the original testing, the analysts involved in processing and reading the retest 
sample under ETX-260831-0275, Gabrielle Surber and Elysse Nioupin, confirmed no 
deviations from standard procedures.
 
Like the original testing under ETX-260811-0261, the retest sample under ETX-260831-
0275 was processed following comparable disinfection procedures, the cleanroom 
environment, the ISO 5 BSC utilized (E001316), and employing consistent aseptic 
techniques. All reagents and supplies used for the retest sample under ETX-260831-0275 
were stored according to the suppliers’ recommendations, and their integrity was visually 
verified before utilization. Moreover, each reagent and supply had a valid expiration date.
 
For the retest sample, the culture media used were within their expiry period. During the 
testing for the membrane filtration method, the negative control media bottles were handled, 
processed, and incubated under the same conditions as the test samples. No microbial 
growth was observed in the corresponding negative controls, confirming the integrity of the 
test procedure.
 
Additionally, the retest sample was cleaned, transferred to the Clean Room, processed, and 
tested as per MICRO-SOP-5 (USP <71> / EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test) on 04 Sep 2026. The 
media bottles were subsequently transferred into designated incubators, E001356 and 
E001357, to initiate incubation identical to the original testing under ETX-260811-0261. 
ETX-260831-0275 passed the sterility test after 14 days of incubation.
 
No laboratory-related errors were identified during the investigation of the passing results 
reported under ETX-260831-0275 or the failing results reported under ETX-260811-0261. 
Therefore, both the original failing result associated with ETX-260811-0261 and the passing 
retest result associated with ETX-260831-0275 are considered valid. Final lot disposition 
remains at the discretion of the client.
