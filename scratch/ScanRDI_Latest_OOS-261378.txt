All analysts involved in the prepping, processing, and reading of the sample; Elysse Nioupin, Sonal Uprety and Varsha 
Subramanian were interviewed, and their answers are recorded throughout this document. 
 
Upon arrival, the sample was stored in accordance with the client’s instructions. Analysts - Elysse Nioupin and Sonal Uprety - 
verified the sample’s integrity throughout both the preparation and processing stages. No leaks or turbidity were observed at 
any point, verifying the integrity of the sample.
 
All reagents and supplies listed in the materials section above were stored according to the suppliers’ recommendations, and 
their integrity was visually verified prior to use. Additionally, all reagents and supplies were within their valid expiration dates. 
 
During the sample preparation, Sonal Uprety disinfected the sample using acidified bleach and placed them into a pre-
disinfected storage bin. On 11Jun 2026, prior to sample processing, Sonal Uprety performed a second disinfection with 
acidified bleach, allowing a minimum contact time of 10 minutes before transferring the sample into the cleanroom suite. 
 
A final disinfection step was completed immediately before the sample was introduced into the ISO 5 BSC E001313, located 
in the middle ISO 7 room, Suite 115A, for processing. All activities were performed in accordance with SOP 2.600.023, Rapid 
Scan RDI® Test Using FIFU Method.
 
The cleanroom used for pre-labelling and filtration steps (Suite 115) comprises three interconnected sections: the innermost 
ISO 7 cleanroom (115B), which connects to the middle ISO 7 buffer room (115A), and then to the outermost ISO 8 anteroom 
(115). A positive air pressure system is maintained throughout the suite to ensure controlled, unidirectional airflow from 115B 
through 115A and into115. 
 
Similarly, the cleanroom used for changeover procedures (Suite 117) comprises three interconnected sections: the innermost 
ISO 7 cleanroom (117B), which connects to the middle ISO 7 buffer room (117A), and then to the outermost ISO 8 anteroom 
(117). A positive air pressure system is maintained throughout the suite to ensure controlled, unidirectional airflow from 117B 
through 117A and into 117.
 
The ISO 5 BSC E001313, located in the middle ISO 7 room (Suite 115A) was used for the pre-labelling and filtration steps,  
while BSC E001309 located in the middle ISO room (Suite 117A) was used for changeover steps. They were thoroughly 
cleaned and disinfected prior to each procedure in accordance with SOP 2.600.018 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for 
Microbiology). Additionally, BSCs E001309 and E001313 were certified and approved by both the Engineering and Quality 
Assurance teams. Sample processing and changeover were conducted in the ISO 5 BSC E001313 in the middle ISO 7 room, 
Suite 115A and middle ISO 5 BSC E001309, Suite 117A, respectively by Sonal Uprety on 11Jun2026.
 
The sample was read by Varsha Subramanian who confirmed that equipment E001040 was set up in accordance with SOP 
2.700.004 (Scan RDI® System – Operations [Standard C3 Quality Check and Microscope Setup and Maintenance]). The 
negative and positive controls for analyst Sonal Uprety, along with the daily control for instrument E001040, yielded expected 
results.

Phase I Summary (continued):  
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
JEsqueda , 9/30/2025, 4:15:53 PM
On 11Jun2026, a rapid sterility test was performed on the sample using the ScanRDI method. The sample was 
initially prepared by Elysse Nioupin, processed by Sonal Uprety and read by Varsha Subramanian. The test 
results revealed the presence of rod-shaped morphology (see Table 1).
 
The environmental monitoring data summary related to the sample under ETX-260605-0187 is attached to this 
OOS report (see Table 2).
 
No microbial growth was observed on the left or right personnel touch plates associated with the processor, 
Sonal Uprety. In addition, surface contact plates and settling plates collected from BSCs E001313 and 
E001309 showed no microbial growth. Also, no growth was observed on the weekly surface sampling and air 
sampling plates collected from cleanroom suite 115 on 11Jun2026. No growth was also observed on the 
weekly surface sampling plates collected from cleanroom suite 117 on same day. 
 
However, 1 CFU (ETX-260622-0279) was recovered from the weekly active air sampling of  ISO 7 Suite 117B 
and identified as Staphylococcus epidermidis (Gram (+) cocci). Although microbial growth was detected within 
the innermost Suite 117B, contamination originating from this area is unlikely to affect samples processed 
within ISO 5 Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC) E001309, which is located in the middle ISO 7 Suite 117A. The 
ISO 5 environment of the BSC also provides a higher level of environmental control and product protection, 
thereby minimizing the potential impact of contamination detected in Suite 117B.
 
Furthermore, the colony morphology of the microorganisms recovered from the cleanroom environment differed 
from that of the microorganism isolated from the test sample. This difference in morphology indicates that the 
environmental monitoring recovery and the test sample contamination were separate and unrelated events.
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection, using H₂O₂, of the cleanroom (ISO 7) and its containing Biosafety Cabinets 
(BSCs, ISO 5) were performed on 31May2026, as per SOP 2.600.018 Cleaning and Disinfection Procedure. It 
was documented that all H₂O₂ indicators passed. 
 
Analysis of a six-month sample history Perrone Pharmacy (E01504) indicates that Eagle Analytical processed 
146 samples for sterility tests using the Scan RDI platform, and there has been no prior incidence of positive 
results during this period. 
 
To assess the potential for sample-to-sample contamination contributing to the positive results, a 
comprehensive review was conducted of all samples processed on the same day. No other sample processed 
by the same analyst on day of testing were found to be positive for microbial growth. These findings suggest 
that cross-contamination between samples is unlikely.
 
Review of sample lot history indicates that there have been no other submissions of specific sample lot- 
20260602@2 from the same client for ScanRDI or any other sterility tests.
 
Based on the observations outlined above, it is highly unlikely that the failing results were due to reagents, 
supplies, the cleanroom environment, the process, or analyst involvement. Consequently, the possibility of 
laboratory error contributing to this failure is minimal. Therefore, the original test result is deemed valid.
