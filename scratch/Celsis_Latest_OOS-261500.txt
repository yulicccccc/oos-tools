All analysts involved in the prepping, processing, aliquoting, and reading of the sample – Alex Saravia, Guanchen Li, and 
America Alanis were interviewed comprehensively. Their answers are recorded throughout this document.
 
Upon arrival, the sample was stored in accordance with the Client’s instructions. Analyst Alex Saravia verified the integrity of 
the sample throughout both the preparation and processing stages. No leaks or turbidity were observed at any point, verifying 
the integrity of the sample.
 
All reagents and supplies mentioned in the material section above were stored according to the suppliers’ recommendations, 
and their integrity was visually verified before utilization. Moreover, all reagents and supplies had valid expiration dates. The 
functionality of all equipment was confirmed by reviewing data generated by our comprehensive in-house continuous 
monitoring system.
 
The cleanroom used for processing procedures (Suite 115) comprises three interconnected sections: the innermost ISO 7 
cleanroom (115B), which connects to the middle ISO 7 buffer room (115A), and then to the outermost ISO 8 anteroom (115). 
A positive air pressure system is maintained throughout the suite to ensure controlled, unidirectional airflow from 115B 
through 115A and into 115.
 
The cleanroom used for aliquoting procedures (L-Suite) comprises four interconnected rooms: the innermost ISO 7 cleanroom 
(CR145), which connects to the middle ISO 7 buffer cleanroom (CR144), followed by ISO 8 anteroom CR143 and the 
outermost ISO 8 room CR142. A positive air pressure system is maintained throughout the suite to ensure controlled, 
unidirectional airflow from CR145 through CR144 and CR143 into CR142.
 
The ISO 5 Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC) E001314, located in the innermost ISO 7 room, (115B), and ISO 5 BSC E001937, 
located in the middle ISO 7 buffer room (CR144) were thoroughly cleaned and disinfected prior to their respective procedures 
in accordance with SOP 2.600.018 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology). Additionally, the BSCs used 
throughout testing, E001314 for sample processing and E001937 for the aliquoting step, were certified and approved by both 
the Engineering and Quality Assurance teams. Sample processing was conducted in the ISO 5 BSC E001314 in the 
innermost ISO 7 room, (115B) by Guanchen Li on 19Jun26, and the aliquoting step was conducted in the ISO 5 BSC 
E001937 in the middle ISO 7 buffer room (CR144) by America Alanis on 26Jun26 as per SOP 2.600.059.
 
On 18Jun26, the sample vials for ETX-260617-0492 were received from the Sample Submissions team and brought into the 
Sterile Microbiology lab. Upon arrival, each sample vial was sprayed with an acidified bleach disinfectant, placed into pre-
disinfected bins, and allowed a 10-minute contact time.
 
On 19Jun26, the secondary disinfection happened in the ISO 8 anteroom (115), where the vials were again treated with 
acidified bleach and provided a 10‑minute contact time before processing. Subsequently, the vials were moved into innermost 
ISO 7 cleanroom (115B). Inside this cleanroom, the processing analyst, Guanchen Li, performed a final disinfection step, 
allowing an additional 10-minute contact time. Once fully disinfected, the vials were transferred into the ISO 5 BSC E001314.

Once transferred into the ISO 5 BSC, the vials were placed on the disinfected working surface of the BSC 
E001314 and as each vial was aseptically resuspended with client specified 6cc of bacteriostatic water. After 
resuspension, the contents of vials were aseptically withdrawn from with sterile needle and syringe, and tested in 
accordance with SOP 2.600.059 (Celsis Sterility Testing). Following testing, the media bottles were subsequently 
transferred into designated incubators, E001356 and E001357, to initiate incubation.
 
Upon completion of incubation on 26Jun26, both TSB & FTM bottles were disinfected and transferred to the middle 
ISO 7 buffer room (CR144) for aliquoting step per SOP 2.600.059 (Celsis Sterility Testing). In CR144, the media 
bottles were disinfected one more time before transferring them to the ISO 5 BSC E001937 located in CR144. In 
ISO 5 BSC E001937, the sample was aliquoted into assay cuvettes by analyst America Alanis. After aliquoting, 
Celsis Sterility Reading was performed in accordance with SOP 2.600.059 by analyst America Alanis.
 
Following the reading, sample ETX-260617-0492 was found to yield a positive reading in the FTM media bottle. 
The average Relative Luminescence Units (RLU) from the duplicate reading tubes, originating from the FTM 
sample bottle, exceeded the average RLU of the FTM negative control, confirming a positive result. The %CV from 
the duplicate reading tubes for the positive FTM bottle were well within the specification (< 30%). Additionally, all 
Daily Controls, including the Instrument Blank, Reagent Blank, and ATP Positive Control, were within the defined 
specifications, each with a %CV below 30%.
 
Following the OOS result, the positive FTM bottle for ETX-260617-0492 was submitted for Differential Staining and 
Microbial Identification under ETX-260626-0459, where the organism was identified as Bacillus paralicheniformis.
 
The culture media utilized were within their expiry period. The negative culture media bottles for the direct 
inoculation method for the original culture were handled, processed, and incubated in a manner identical to that of 
actual samples. No microbial growth was observed in the corresponding negative control.
 
Upon review of the environmental monitoring (EM) results associated with the processing and aliquoting activities, 
limited microbial recovery was observed during personnel and settling plate monitoring. During the processing 
step, 1 CFU was recovered from Settling Plate 2 on the day after testing. The isolate was submitted for microbial 
identification under Sample ID ETX-260702-0300 and was identified as Staphylococcus warneri. During the 
aliquoting step, 4 CFUs were recovered from the analyst's left-touch personnel monitoring plate on the day prior to 
testing. The isolate was submitted for microbial identification under Sample ID ETX-260706-0280 and was 
identified as Bacillus paralicheniformis.The analysts involved in the activities confirmed full adherence to all 
applicable cleaning, disinfection, and aseptic processing procedures as defined in SOP 2.600.018, Cleaning and 
Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology, SOP 2.600.059, Celsis Sterility Testing, and SOP 2.600.008,  USP <71>
/EP 2.6.1 Sterility Test.
 
Review of routine environmental monitoring data identified additional low-level microbial recovery within the 
surrounding ISO 8 areas. During weekly active air monitoring, 2 CFUs were detected in ISO 8 Room 115. The 
isolate was submitted for microbial identification under Sample ID ETX-260615-0432 and was identified as 
Staphylococcus epidermidis. In the week preceding the aliquoting activity, weekly active air monitoring detected 3 
CFUs in CR143 Section II (ISO 8) and 11 CFUs in CR142 (ISO 8). The isolates were submitted for microbial 
identification under Sample IDs ETX-260706-0305 and ETX-260706-0300. Identifications for ETX-260706-0305 
included Micrococcus luteus, Staphylococcus haemolyticus, and Auricoccus indicus, while isolates recovered 
under ETX-260706-0300 were identified as Micrococcus luteus, Staphylococcus capitis, and Tremateia 
guiyangensis.

Additionally, during weekly surface monitoring performed in the week preceding the aliquoting activity, 2 
CFUs were recovered from the sink located in CR142 (ISO 8). The isolate was submitted for microbial 
identification under Sample ID ETX-260706-0295 and was identified as Gram-positive rods. Furthermore, 5 
CFUs were recovered from the sink in CR142, the outermost ISO 8 room, during monitoring conducted in 
the week following testing. The microorganisms were also submitted for microbial identification under 
Sample ID ETX-260713-0361 and were identified as Gram (+) coccobacilli and Gram (+) cocci, the 
outermost ISO 8 room, during monitoring conducted in the week following testing.
 
While sporadic microbial recovery was observed during routine environmental monitoring, it is important to 
note that the microorganisms identified were recovered primarily from the surrounding ISO 8 background 
environment. The processing and aliquoting operations were conducted exclusively within a qualified ISO 5 
BSC located in innermost ISO 7 Room (115B) and middle buffer ISO 7 Room (CR144) respectively, which 
provides a substantially higher level of environmental control than the surrounding room. Importantly, 
environmental monitoring results from the critical work area, including work surfaces and analyst glove 
monitoring, did not demonstrate contamination during the execution of the activities. 
 
The absence of recovery from these critical monitoring locations indicates that no viable contamination 
pathway was established between the ISO 8 background environment and the ISO 5 processing area. 
Furthermore, the lack of contamination in other concurrently tested samples, combined with analyst 
adherence to established aseptic techniques and cleaning procedures, provides additional assurance that 
the testing environment remained in a state of control and that the integrity of the processing and aliquoting 
activities was not adversely impacted by the microorganisms recovered during routine environmental 
monitoring.
 
Monthly cleaning and disinfection of the outermost ISO 8 Anteroom, the middle ISO 7 Buffer room, the 
innermost ISO 7 cleanroom, and its containing ISO 5 Biosafety Cabinets for Suite 115 and L-suite were 
performed on 31May26, as per SOP 2.600.018 (Cleaning and Disinfecting Procedure for Microbiology) 
During both cleaning cycles, it was documented that all H₂O₂ indicators passed. This confirms the efficient 
monthly cleaning of all parts of Cleanrooms for Suite 115 and L-suite.
 
Analyzing a 6-month sample history for Revive Rx Pharmacy (E00927), this specific analyte "Tesamorelin 
12 mg per Vial" has had no prior failures using Celsis sterility testing during this period.
 
To assess the potential for sample-to-sample contamination contributing to the positive results, a 
comprehensive review was conducted of all samples processed on the same day. All other samples 
processed by the analyst and other analysts that day tested negative. These findings suggest that cross-
contamination between samples is highly unlikely.
 
Based on the observations outlined above, it is unlikely that the failing results were due to reagents, 
supplies, the cleanroom environment, the process, or analyst involvement. Consequently, the possibility of 
laboratory error contributing to this failure is minimal and the original result is deemed to be valid.
